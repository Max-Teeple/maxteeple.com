Internet at the camera

Plan on at least 5–10 Mbps of upload for 1080p, or 3–4 Mbps for 720p. Run a speed test standing where the camera will sit, not only in the clubhouse. Cloudflare’s speed test or a phone speed-test app is enough.

  • Clubhouse Wi-Fi plus an outdoor access point or a point-to-point bridge aimed at the tee
  • A cellular hotspot or weatherproof 4G/5G router at the camera
  • Starlink, when the course has no usable wired or cellular path

Where to point it

The usual first spots are the 1st tee, the 18th green, and the range. Face the camera so the sun is behind it in the hours you care about. A privacy sign at the tee that says the hole is on a public or unlisted live stream is worth putting up before the first broadcast.

YouTube can be set to a scheduled premiere, and a managed camera can be left powered so it reconnects to the same stream key. All-day operation needs continuous power (outlet, PoE, or solar) and shade or a light-colored enclosure so the camera does not shut down from heat.

YouTube Live link

  1. Verify the channel and enable live streaming. The first approval can take up to 24 hours.
  2. Create a persistent stream key, not a one-time key.
  3. Set the stream to Unlisted or Public, and turn on DVR.
  4. Copy the watch URL or the channel live URL and paste it in the Go Live wizard.

Twitch alternative: use the Creator Dashboard stream key and paste your twitch.tv channel URL instead. Vimeo and a public HLS address also embed.

Clubhouse TV setup

Each course has a Clubhouse TV link in the course studio, with a copy button and a QR code. The page is full screen: no menus, no chat, a larger overlay, and it asks the browser to stay awake.

  1. Recommended: a streaming stick (Fire TV or Android TV) running a kiosk browser such as Fully Kiosk Browser or another Android TV kiosk app. Open the Clubhouse TV link, allow autoplay, and turn the stick’s screensaver off. Silk and Android TV browsers both run this page at 1080p and 720p.
  2. Fallback: the smart TV’s YouTube app, opened to the same YouTube live. Viewers see the broadcast without the LiveView overlay or the Back soon slide.
  3. Last resort: a laptop on HDMI. Open the Clubhouse TV link in Chrome or Firefox and leave the machine set not to sleep.

The page retries with a longer pause if the stream drops, and it reloads itself about every 20 minutes so a stuck picture recovers. If the browser cannot keep the screen awake, the kiosk app’s “stay awake” setting covers it.

Troubleshooting

YouTube will not let the channel go live

New channels have to be verified. That wait can be up to 24 hours. The rest of the wizard can be saved and resumed.

Test my link does nothing

Paste the watch URL, the channel /live URL, a Twitch channel, a Vimeo video, or a public .m3u8 or .mp4 address. A Studio dashboard URL will not embed.

The picture freezes at the tee

Test upload speed at the camera. Office Wi-Fi often does not reach the tee box.

The camera drops out in the afternoon

Check power, the PoE link, and heat inside the enclosure. A dark enclosure in full sun will shut a camera down.

The course stays offline after you go live

The link has to be approved, then marked live in the course studio. Pending approval is a normal step.
